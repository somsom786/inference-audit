Strongest verified result

Inkling-2-Mini: place the looped core on the GPU.

Loop-aware expert placement beat llama.cpp auto-fit by +36.5% to +37.8% decode and +54.8% to +55.2% at 8K prefill. It ran 2.1x faster decode and 8.1–8.2x faster prefill than --cpu-moe, and passed 6 of 6 local acceptance checks (not an official registry recipe; vision, concurrency and production infra unverified).

Published external research (Sybil Solutions)

Published results from the wider inference optimization ecosystem

Inference engineering is producing large gains across very different hardware. These are independent, published results from Sybil Solutions, shown for context so you can see what systematic work can achieve. They are not our measurements.

Published external research

Intel Arc B390 iGPU

297 → 628 tok/s

256K prefill, 2.1x faster. Startup fell from 113 s to 43 s and KV capacity rose from 264K to about 1.05M tokens (4 x 256K).

Business relevance: faster long-context ingestion, quicker restarts and more concurrent long sessions on the same laptop-class hardware.

Source: local-ai-registry PR #453

Published external research

Intel Arc Pro B70

91.2 vs 25.0 tok/s

Single-user decode, Qwen3.8-27B on vLLM with custom XPU kernels versus llama.cpp Q4_K_M on the same card. This is a cross-stack comparison, not a like-for-like tuning gain.

Business relevance: the serving stack can matter as much as the card, so compare stacks before buying hardware.

Source: trellis-serve XPU README

Published external research

NVIDIA RTX 3090

45.5 → 65.6 tok/s

Single-stream decode with native multi-token prediction on Flash-Next EXL3 at 256K context, a 44.2% gain.

Business relevance: speculative decoding can lift single-user speed on hardware you may already own.

Source: trellis-serve CUDA README

Hypothetical economic scenario

What could more capacity be worth?

Illustrative only: at $50,000/month of GPU spend, a 30% gain in qualified throughput could use 23.1% fewer GPU hours for the same useful work. That is up to about $138,462/year if 100% of the spend can scale down. If your bill is fixed, the same gain gives headroom while cash savings remain zero.

Qualified throughput means correct tasks finished within your latency and context requirements. Use the improvement measured on your workload. These are not measured customer savings.

Controlled optimization pilot

Compare an approved staging change with neighboring controls. Check latency, memory, context and correctness before modeling any possible financial impact.

  1. Agree a representative workloadReal traffic shape, service targets and an approved endpoint.
  2. Measure a read-only baselineNo changes to production servers.
  3. Identify hypothesesRank the changes most likely to matter.
  4. Test approved candidatesStaging only, against neighboring controls.
  5. Validate correctness, latency and contextA faster result that fails a target does not count.
  6. Quantify conditional financial impactSavings only where spend can actually shrink.
  7. Deliver reproducible findingsRaw evidence, flags and a method you can rerun.
